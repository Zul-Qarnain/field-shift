Why

  • Region: the claim that a small region gives 6–10× better odds is wrong for 2026. The official BD guide gives each city 1 global nomination per 10 submitted projects ("twenty submissions means two nominations; every further ten adds one") [guide]. The 2025 nominee list shows how this plays out: Dhaka got 7 nominees, and the other regions got 1–4 each [n2025]. So the baseline odds are about 10% everywhere. For a strong team, a big pool is actually safer, because there are 12 slots to finish inside instead of 1 (the "law of large numbers" effect in section D). A small region only helps a middle-of-the-pack team.
  • Challenge: Earth-observation data apps are the most common winning type (9 of 30 winners in 2023–25), and agriculture has won in both of the last two years [nasa2024][w2025]. The judges' score sheet gives 20 points each to Impact and Relevance ("NASA-data-first") [guide]. Field Shift and Trend Detective let you put NASA data at the centre, tell a Bangladesh climate story and show real statistical depth.
  • Keep a creative backup: Bangladesh's global record comes from storytelling and education projects (Voyagers 2023 Best Storytelling; 2 of the 3 BD finalists in 2025 were on the storytelling challenge) [nasa2023][f2025]. Whichever challenge you pick, the 240-s and 30-s videos should tell a human story.

Key risks

  • Full challenge statements only come out on 28 Oct. Re-rank then; the summaries may hide requirements.
  • Rule conflict: the global FAQ says "Teams are not allowed to begin working on the challenges prior to the hackathon" [faq]. The BD guide asks for a Video 2 with a working demo by 1 Nov [guide], and the other AI's build guide tells you to pre-build caches and code. Ask your Local Lead in writing what counts as prep.
  • Field Shift is popular in BD: 5 of BD's 27 nominees in 2025 were on the agriculture challenge, and none became finalists [n2025][f2025]. To stand out you need more depth than a dashboard.
  • Registration counts are individuals on spaceappschallenge.org as of 25 Sep and will change. The number of submitted projects per city, which decides the slots, is only known at the deadline.

D · Region math: probability of advancing (global nomination)

Rule used: slots = max(1, floor(S/10)), where S = submitted projects in the city FACT for ≥10 [guide]. The "max 1" for S<10 is an ASSUMPTION. S = registered participants ÷ average registrants per team × submission rate. Default 4 registrants per team is an ASSUMPTION. The default 61% submission rate is the 2025 global team→project ratio FACT [metrics].

Skill model (ESTIMATE): each competitor's quality ~ N(0,1), and your team sits at the chosen top-x% quantile. The judged score = quality + noise σ. P(advance) = P(fewer than slots competitors outscore you), computed exactly when σ=0 (binomial) and by 3,000-run Monte Carlo when σ>0.

The saw-tooth comes from floor rounding: a city just below a multiple of 10 loses a slot. Elite teams (top 2–5%) get safer as the pool grows. Mid teams (top 20–30%) only have a real chance in small pools.

Region recommendation: Register where you actually live and can attend (Dhaka if you're Dhaka-based). Under the official 1-per-10 rule, baseline odds are about 10% in every city. A small city only helps if you'd be a middle-ranked team, and your goal is a global award, which needs a top-few-% project anyway. For a top-5% team, the default model gives ≈98% in Dhaka vs ≈42–51% in the 1-slot cities. Even if you'd be top 2% in Barisal because fewer strong teams go there, it's ≈75%. Dhaka also hosts the national mainstage (AIUB) with mentors on site [guide].
Honest caveats. (1) The model assumes team quality is spread the same way in every city. If Dhaka has many elite university teams, your percentile there is worse. (2) S is only known at the deadline, and registrations keep growing until 15 Nov. (3) The slot rule for cities with fewer than 10 submissions is unknown. (4) Registering outside your home city may conflict with local expectations (Chattogram text, and "register for the city you compete from"). Barisal is virtual-only, so it needs no travel, but check with the Local Lead first. (5) There's no registration-to-submission dropout data for BD cities; the 61% is a global figure.

How to win: what set past global winners apart

1 · A working, interactive prototype built on real NASA data

Almost every 2023–2024 winner description is a working artefact driven by named NASA data. Examples: "a web app that identifies satellite risk zones using real-time data on Near-Earth Objects" (WMPGang), "assessed planting conditions using surface soil moisture and evapotranspiration data, then created an app" (NVS-knot), and "a website that integrates moonquake data… ALSEP Apollo experiments data" (Spacebee) [nasa2024][nasa2023]. The BD guide: "Show the thing working. A slideshow about a thing is not a demo of a thing." [guide]

2 · Real modelling or analysis, not just a dashboard

Storm Prophet used "LSTM models" (2023 Best Use of Data), 42 QuakeHeroes "a deep neural network model… advanced signal processing" (2024 Best Use of Technology), and Asteroid Destroyer "machine learning techniques to map exoplanets" [nasa2023][nasa2024]. On the score sheet, Validity 20 means "scientifically robust, using validated data and methods… clear performance metrics" [guide].

3 · A human or local story on top of the tech

TerraTales told climate stories of Egypt, Brazil and Germany with an AI forecast plus a game. Voyagers built "an interactive web-based game that tells the imperative story" of water. Arcobaleno made NASA images accessible to blind users through sonification [nasa2024][nasa2023]. The Impact criterion asks: "Who benefits? How many? How measurable?" [guide]

4 · The 30-second video and the project page are everything globally

"Video 3… is all a NASA judge on the other side of the world will ever see." "Everything global judges see lives on your NASA project page." English subtitles are required. The repo must be public, every NASA and partner dataset listed, and every AI tool named along with its prompts [guide]. NASA may remove nominations from teams that don't cite resources [legal].

5 · Take the cheap points

Challenge named, repo open and page submitted are worth 1 point each. The +5% women-participation bonus applies to the whole score, so a team with at least one woman member gets it [guide]. Mixed skills beat six developers: "the strongest teams are the mixed ones" (developer + designer + domain researcher + storyteller) [guide].

6 · Pick the award category deliberately

There are ten categories, and local judges tick which ones they nominate you for [guide]. For Field Shift or Trend Detective, aim at Best Use of Data, Local Impact or Galactic Impact; agriculture apps won Best Use of Data and Galactic Impact in 2024 [nasa2024].

7 · Plan around the 240-s pitch

The prescreening video (1 Oct) decides whether you get a seat, and local judging happens in "240 Seconds of Glory". Name every member, stay under 240 s ("over-length videos get cut off"), and state your NASA data use out loud [guide].
